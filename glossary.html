Photonics glossary

40 terms you will run into when you research photonics ETFs, explained without the jargon.

ABCDEFHILNOPRSTUW
A
Active ETF
A fund where a portfolio manager picks the holdings and sizes them, instead of following an index. Most photonics ETFs are active. How photonics ETFs differ →
AUM (assets under management)
The total market value of everything a fund holds. Bigger funds usually trade with tighter spreads. See AUM for every fund →
B
Bid-ask spread
The gap between the price buyers are offering and the price sellers are asking. Small, new ETFs often have wider spreads, which is a hidden cost when you trade.
C
Co-packaged optics (CPO)
A design that moves the optical engine out of a plug-in module and puts it right next to the switch chip, shortening the electrical path to cut power use and signal loss. Co-packaged optics explained →
Coherent optics
A long-distance optical transmission method that encodes data in the phase and amplitude of light, used to link data centers and cities.
Continuous-wave (CW) laser
A laser that emits a steady beam. In silicon photonics modules, a CW laser provides the light and a separate modulator adds the data.
D
Daily reset
How leveraged ETFs work: they rebalance every day to hit a multiple of that single day's move, which makes longer-term returns drift from the simple multiple. See the EUVX profile →
Datacom
Optical links inside and between data centers, as opposed to telecom links run by phone and cable carriers.
Directed energy
Defense systems that use high-energy lasers to disable targets such as drones.
DSP (digital signal processor)
A chip inside an optical module that cleans up and retimes the signal. It is one of the most power-hungry parts of a transceiver.
E
EML (electro-absorption modulated laser)
A laser chip with a built-in modulator, widely used in high-speed data center transceivers. Supply has been tight during the AI buildout.
EUV lithography
Extreme ultraviolet lithography: printing chip patterns with 13.5-nanometer light. ASML is the only company that makes the machines. See the EUV fund →
Expense ratio
The yearly fee a fund charges, shown as a percent of your investment. A 0.75% expense ratio costs $75 a year on $10,000. Compare fees →
F
Fiber optics
Thin strands of glass that carry data as pulses of light over long distances with very little loss.
H
Hyperscaler
One of the giant cloud companies, such as Amazon, Microsoft, Alphabet and Meta, whose data center spending drives most demand for optical components.
I
Index ETF
A fund that follows a published rulebook instead of a manager's picks. PHOX and PRAM are the index funds on our list. See PHOX →
Indium phosphide (InP)
A compound semiconductor material used to make the lasers and detectors in optical communications. Silicon cannot efficiently produce light, so InP supplies it.
Interconnect
Any link that moves data between chips, servers or racks. Optical interconnects do it with light instead of copper.
L
Laser
A device that produces a narrow, intense beam of light of a single color. Lasers are the light source for nearly everything in photonics.
Leveraged ETF
A fund that uses swaps or other derivatives to target a multiple, such as 2x, of an index or fund's daily return. How photonics ETFs differ →
LiDAR
Light detection and ranging: measuring distance by timing laser pulses. Used in vehicles, robots and mapping.
Linear pluggable optics (LPO)
A transceiver design that removes the DSP chip to save power, relying on the switch chip to handle the signal.
Lithography
The step in chipmaking that uses light to print circuit patterns onto silicon wafers.
N
NAV (net asset value)
The per-share value of what an ETF holds. An ETF's market price can trade slightly above or below its NAV.
Non-diversified fund
A legal label for a fund allowed to put large shares of its assets into a few holdings. Most photonics ETFs carry it.
O
Optical circuit switch (OCS)
A switch that routes light directly from one fiber to another without converting it to an electrical signal.
Optical transceiver
A small module that converts electrical signals into light and back, so data can travel over fiber. Data centers use millions of them. What is a photonics ETF? →
P
Photonic integrated circuit (PIC)
A chip that puts several optical parts, such as lasers, modulators and detectors, on a single piece of material.
Photonics
The science and industry of generating, guiding and detecting light. It covers lasers, fiber, optical chips, sensors and lithography. What is a photonics ETF? →
Pluggable optics
Transceiver modules that plug into the front of a switch and can be swapped out. The standard design in data centers today.
Private holdings
Shares of companies that do not trade on a stock exchange. A few photonics ETFs hold some, valued by estimate. Photonics ETF risks →
Pure play
A company that gets most of its revenue, often defined as at least 50%, from a single theme such as photonics.
Put credit spread
An options trade that sells one put and buys a cheaper, lower-strike put. It collects income up front and has a known maximum loss. Photonics income ETFs →
R
Return of capital
Part of a fund's distribution that is your own money coming back instead of income or gains. It lowers your cost basis. Photonics income ETFs →
S
Silicon photonics
Building optical components on silicon wafers with chipmaking tools, which allows high volumes and tight integration with electronics.
Special purpose vehicle (SPV)
A separate legal entity set up to hold a single investment, often used to give funds exposure to a private company.
T
Thematic ETF
A fund built around a single idea or technology instead of a broad market index. Every fund on this site is thematic.
Total return swap
A contract with a bank that pays a fund the return of a stock or index without the fund owning it. It adds counterparty risk. Photonics ETF risks →
U
UCITS ETF
A fund built under European Union rules and sold to investors in Europe and the UK. Europe has its own photonics UCITS ETF.
W
Wavelength
The color of light, measured in nanometers. Different wavelengths can carry separate data streams down the same fiber.
