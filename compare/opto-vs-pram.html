The short version

PRAM is cheaper to own, at 0.65% a year versus 0.99%. PRAM is the bigger fund, with $5M in assets. PRAM has been around longer, launching on August 26, 2026.

OPTO

The Tuttle Capital Photonics Income Blast ETF is the income version of FOTO. It keeps exposure to the same universe of photonics companies and adds an options strategy that sells put spreads on those stocks, with the goal of paying out the premium as weekly distributions. The option strategy is not designed to protect you when photonics stocks fall. Distributions can include return of capital, can shrink and can be suspended, and the fund's share price can drop even while it pays out. It is also brand new and very small.

PRAM

The Defiance Memory & Photonics ETF is a hybrid, not a pure photonics fund. It tracks the Solactive Memory and Photonics Index: the 20 largest eligible memory and photonics companies, equal-weighted at each quarterly rebalance. Coherent, Lumentum, Zhongji Innolight and Applied Optoelectronics sit next to Micron, SK Hynix, Seagate and Western Digital. Roughly half of PRAM is memory and data storage, so it will not track the optical stocks as closely as the pure photonics funds. The fund gets its index exposure through a total return swap, not by holding the 20 stocks directly.

Lower fees and more assets are helpful, but they are not the whole story. The bigger question is which strategy matches what you want to own. This comparison is not a recommendation to buy or sell either fund.

FAQ

What is the difference between OPTO and PRAM?

OPTO is the Tuttle Capital Photonics Income Blast ETF from Tuttle Capital (fiber & optical networking), while PRAM is the Defiance Memory & Photonics ETF from Defiance (ai datacenter interconnect). They differ in strategy, fees and size.

Which is cheaper, OPTO or PRAM?

PRAM has the lower expense ratio: 0.65% vs 0.99%.

Which is bigger, OPTO or PRAM?

PRAM has more assets: $5M vs $554K.
