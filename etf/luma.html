LUMA at a glance

The KraneShares Photonic and Optical ETF targets public and private companies worldwide that build optical interconnects, transceivers, fiber-optic cable and other light-based infrastructure for AI networking.

  • Size: about $9M in assets, #5 of 9 photonics ETFs, or roughly 1% of all the money in the funds we track.
  • Cost: 1.00% a year, the highest fee on the list. The group average is 0.69%.
  • How it is run: Actively managed.
  • Concentration: the three largest positions add up to about 24% of the fund (Ayar Labs (private), Aixtron, Coherent).
  • Track record: 4 months of trading history, so there is no long-term record to judge yet.
Holds private companiesSome positions are private companies valued by estimate, not a daily market price.
China-listed exposureHolds or references stocks listed in mainland China.

Worth knowing

LUMA can hold private companies, IPOs and derivatives, and it carries one of the higher expense ratios in the group. That flexibility cuts both ways, so it is worth reading how the fund values and sizes its private positions.

How LUMA invests

LUMA is the most international fund in the group and the one with the most private-company exposure. Its largest position in the issuer's November 2026 holdings file was Ayar Labs, a private developer of optical chip-to-chip links, at about 12% of assets.

The public side of the portfolio spreads across Europe and Asia as well as the U.S.: Aixtron in Germany, Soitec in France, Eoptolink in China, Tower Semiconductor in Israel, LandMark in Taiwan and Nokia, next to Coherent, MACOM and Lumentum.

KraneShares describes the process as rules-based and research-driven. Position sizes are flatter than at LYTE or LAZR, so no single listed stock dominates.

Read the prospectus and the latest holdings on the KraneShares website.

What LUMA holds

The top three positions make up about 24% of LUMA. Its published top holdings overlap most with LAZR (5 shared names), PHOX (4 shared names), LYTE (3 shared names). See every stock the photonics ETFs own and how much they overlap.

LUMA fees

LUMA charges 1.00% a year. On a $10,000 position that is about $100 a year, $500 over five years and $1,000 over ten, before any gains or losses. The same $10,000 in the cheapest photonics ETF (EUV at 0.35%) would cost $350 over ten years, and the most expensive (LUMA at 1.00%) would cost $1,000.

LUMA size

With about $9M in assets, LUMA ranks #5 of 9 photonics ETFs. That is small. Small funds can trade with wider bid-ask spreads, and issuers sometimes close funds that do not gather assets, so it is worth checking the spread and using limit orders.

Who LUMA may fit

It may fit if

  • You want the widest global spread, including European photonics suppliers
  • You want meaningful private-company exposure to optical chip startups
  • You prefer flatter position sizes over one or two giant holdings

It may not fit if

  • Fees matter most to you; this is the most expensive fund on the list
  • You want a large, heavily traded fund; LUMA is still small
  • You are not comfortable with a private company as the top holding

General education only, not a recommendation. Your own goals, timeline and risk tolerance decide what fits.

LUMA risks to understand

  • A private company as the largest position is valued by estimate, not a market price
  • Small fund size can mean wider bid-ask spreads
  • The highest fee in the group is a guaranteed drag on returns
  • Like every fund on this site, LUMA is a new, single-theme ETF. Photonics stocks fell hard in October 2026, with several of the biggest names dropping 10% or more in a day. Read our guide to photonics ETF risks before sizing a position.

LUMA FAQ

What is LUMA?

LUMA is the KraneShares Photonic and Optical ETF, a photonics-focused ETF from KraneShares. The KraneShares Photonic and Optical ETF targets public and private companies worldwide that build optical interconnects, transceivers, fiber-optic cable and other light-based infrastructure for AI networking.

What is LUMA's expense ratio?

LUMA charges 1.00% a year, about $100 for every $10,000 invested. That ranks #9 of 9 photonics ETFs, cheapest first.

How big is LUMA?

LUMA has about $9M in assets under management, #5 of 9 photonics ETFs by size.

When did LUMA launch?

LUMA launched on July 15, 2026 and trades on NYSE Arca.

What does LUMA hold?

As of Nov 20, 2026, the largest positions were Ayar Labs (private), Aixtron, Coherent, MACOM Technology, Soitec. Percent of net assets. Holdings change, so check the issuer's website for the current list.

Is LUMA actively managed or an index fund?

LUMA is actively managed, so a portfolio manager chooses the holdings and their sizes instead of following an index.

Does LUMA pay a dividend?

Distribution schedule: Annual. Photonics companies mostly reinvest their cash, so growth-focused photonics ETFs pay little or nothing. Check the issuer's distribution history for actual payments.

Which photonics ETF is most similar to LUMA?

Based on published top holdings, LUMA shares the most names with LAZR (Aixtron, Coherent, Lumentum, Tower Semiconductor). You can compare them side by side on our LUMA vs LAZR page.

How do I buy LUMA?

LUMA trades like a stock on NYSE Arca. You can buy it in most brokerage accounts by searching the ticker LUMA. Newer, smaller ETFs can have wide bid-ask spreads, so many investors use limit orders.

Is LUMA a good investment?

We do not make buy or sell recommendations. Compare its strategy, fees and size with the other photonics ETFs and read the prospectus before deciding.
