EUV at a glance

The Corgi Lithography & Semiconductor Photonics ETF takes a wider angle on photonics, centered on lithography and chipmaking: the light-based tools that print chips, the equipment makers around them and the foundries that use them. Large positions have included ASML, TSMC, Applied Materials, Lam Research and KLA.

  • Size: about $206M in assets, #2 of 9 photonics ETFs, or roughly 30% of all the money in the funds we track.
  • Cost: 0.35% a year, the lowest fee on the list. The group average is 0.69%.
  • How it is run: Actively managed.
  • Concentration: the three largest positions add up to about 30% of the fund (TSMC, ASML, Applied Materials).
  • Track record: 7 months of trading history, so there is no long-term record to judge yet.

Worth knowing

Because it leans on large semiconductor equipment and foundry names, EUV behaves more like a chip-equipment fund than a pure optical networking fund. That can make it steadier than the pure plays, but it is a different bet.

How EUV invests

EUV is named after extreme ultraviolet lithography, the process that uses 13.5-nanometer light to print the smallest features on advanced chips. The fund starts there, with ASML and the foundry that buys the most of its machines, TSMC, then adds the big U.S. equipment makers.

Optical networking is a smaller sleeve. Corning, Lumentum, Coherent and Ciena all appeared in the top ten in the issuer's November 2026 holdings, each at roughly 3% to 4%.

It is the cheapest photonics ETF and the one built from the largest companies, which is a big reason it has gathered more assets than most of the group.

Read the prospectus and the latest holdings on the Corgi Funds website.

What EUV holds

The top three positions make up about 30% of EUV. Its published top holdings overlap most with LYTE (3 shared names), FOTO (3 shared names), LAZR (2 shared names). See every stock the photonics ETFs own and how much they overlap.

EUV fees

EUV charges 0.35% a year. On a $10,000 position that is about $35 a year, $175 over five years and $350 over ten, before any gains or losses. The same $10,000 in the cheapest photonics ETF (EUV at 0.35%) would cost $350 over ten years, and the most expensive (LUMA at 1.00%) would cost $1,000.

EUV size

With about $206M in assets, EUV ranks #2 of 9 photonics ETFs. That puts it among the largest funds in this young category, which usually means tighter spreads and more daily volume.

Who EUV may fit

It may fit if

  • You want the lowest fee among photonics ETFs
  • You want large, established chip-equipment companies with a smaller optical sleeve
  • You want photonics exposure with less small-cap swing

It may not fit if

  • You want a pure optical networking fund; this is mostly chip equipment
  • You already own a semiconductor ETF and want something that does not overlap much with it
  • You want the companies most directly tied to AI data center optics

General education only, not a recommendation. Your own goals, timeline and risk tolerance decide what fits.

EUV risks to understand

  • Heavy overlap with semiconductor equipment funds
  • TSMC and ASML together are close to a quarter of the fund
  • Chip-equipment spending is cyclical and sensitive to export controls
  • Like every fund on this site, EUV is a new, single-theme ETF. Photonics stocks fell hard in October 2026, with several of the biggest names dropping 10% or more in a day. Read our guide to photonics ETF risks before sizing a position.

EUV FAQ

What is EUV?

EUV is the Corgi Lithography & Semiconductor Photonics ETF, a photonics-focused ETF from Corgi Funds. The Corgi Lithography & Semiconductor Photonics ETF takes a wider angle on photonics, centered on lithography and chipmaking: the light-based tools that print chips, the equipment makers around them and the foundries that use them. Large positions have included ASML, TSMC, Applied Materials, Lam Research and KLA.

What is EUV's expense ratio?

EUV charges 0.35% a year, about $35 for every $10,000 invested. That ranks #1 of 9 photonics ETFs, cheapest first.

How big is EUV?

EUV has about $206M in assets under management, #2 of 9 photonics ETFs by size.

When did EUV launch?

EUV launched on May 6, 2026 and trades on Cboe BZX.

What does EUV hold?

As of Nov 20, 2026, the largest positions were TSMC, ASML, Applied Materials, Lam Research, KLA. Percent of net assets. Holdings change, so check the issuer's website for the current list.

Is EUV actively managed or an index fund?

EUV is actively managed, so a portfolio manager chooses the holdings and their sizes instead of following an index.

Does EUV pay a dividend?

Distribution schedule: Annual. Photonics companies mostly reinvest their cash, so growth-focused photonics ETFs pay little or nothing. Check the issuer's distribution history for actual payments.

Which photonics ETF is most similar to EUV?

Based on published top holdings, EUV shares the most names with LYTE (Lumentum, Coherent, Ciena). You can compare them side by side on our EUV vs LYTE page.

How do I buy EUV?

EUV trades like a stock on Cboe BZX. You can buy it in most brokerage accounts by searching the ticker EUV. Newer, smaller ETFs can have wide bid-ask spreads, so many investors use limit orders.

Is EUV a good investment?

We do not make buy or sell recommendations. Compare its strategy, fees and size with the other photonics ETFs and read the prospectus before deciding.
