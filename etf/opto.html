OPTO at a glance

The Tuttle Capital Photonics Income Blast ETF is the income version of FOTO. It keeps exposure to the same universe of photonics companies and adds an options strategy that sells put spreads on those stocks, with the goal of paying out the premium as weekly distributions.

  • Size: about $554K in assets, #9 of 9 photonics ETFs.
  • Cost: 0.99% a year, cheaper than 1 of the other 8 funds. The group average is 0.69%.
  • How it is run: Actively managed, options income.
  • Track record: 49 days of trading history, so there is no long-term record to judge yet.
Options incomeSells options to generate distributions. Payouts are not guaranteed.
Uses swapsPart or all of the exposure comes through total return swaps with a bank instead of shares held directly.

Worth knowing

The option strategy is not designed to protect you when photonics stocks fall. Distributions can include return of capital, can shrink and can be suspended, and the fund's share price can drop even while it pays out. It is also brand new and very small.

How OPTO invests

OPTO holds photonics stock exposure directly or through call options and synthetic long positions, adding up to at least 80% of net assets. Unlike a classic covered-call fund, it does not sell calls against those holdings, so it does not cap the upside on the stock sleeve in the same way.

The income comes from a separate layer of put credit spreads on the same photonics stocks. The fund sells puts close to the current price and buys cheaper puts further below. The difference in premium is the income, and the purchased puts set a known maximum loss on each spread.

Tuttle expects to pay distributions weekly but does not guarantee it. In a selloff the put spreads lose money at the same time the stock sleeve falls, so losses stack instead of offsetting.

Read the prospectus and the latest holdings on the Tuttle Capital website.

What OPTO holds

OPTO holds Options and Treasury bills tied to photonics companies instead of a simple stock basket, and the issuer had not published a full holdings breakdown when we last checked. See the issuer's page for the current list.

OPTO fees

OPTO charges 0.99% a year. On a $10,000 position that is about $99 a year, $495 over five years and $990 over ten, before any gains or losses. The same $10,000 in the cheapest photonics ETF (EUV at 0.35%) would cost $350 over ten years, and the most expensive (LUMA at 1.00%) would cost $1,000.

OPTO size

With about $554K in assets, OPTO ranks #9 of 9 photonics ETFs. That is small. Small funds can trade with wider bid-ask spreads, and issuers sometimes close funds that do not gather assets, so it is worth checking the spread and using limit orders.

Who OPTO may fit

It may fit if

  • You want regular cash payouts from a photonics position
  • You understand that part of each payout may be your own capital coming back
  • You already follow weekly-pay option income ETFs and want one tied to this theme

It may not fit if

  • You want maximum growth from photonics; total return can lag the plain FOTO fund
  • You hold in a taxable account and have not looked at how option-income distributions are taxed
  • You want a fund with a track record; this one launched in October 2026

General education only, not a recommendation. Your own goals, timeline and risk tolerance decide what fits.

OPTO risks to understand

  • Put spreads add losses on top of stock losses in a falling market
  • Distributions are not guaranteed and may be return of capital
  • A 0.99% fee, the second highest on the list, and a tiny asset base
  • Like every fund on this site, OPTO is a new, single-theme ETF. Photonics stocks fell hard in October 2026, with several of the biggest names dropping 10% or more in a day. Read our guide to photonics ETF risks before sizing a position.

OPTO FAQ

What is OPTO?

OPTO is the Tuttle Capital Photonics Income Blast ETF, a photonics-focused ETF from Tuttle Capital. The Tuttle Capital Photonics Income Blast ETF is the income version of FOTO. It keeps exposure to the same universe of photonics companies and adds an options strategy that sells put spreads on those stocks, with the goal of paying out the premium as weekly distributions.

What is OPTO's expense ratio?

OPTO charges 0.99% a year, about $99 for every $10,000 invested. That ranks #8 of 9 photonics ETFs, cheapest first.

How big is OPTO?

OPTO has about $554K in assets under management, #9 of 9 photonics ETFs by size.

When did OPTO launch?

OPTO launched on October 8, 2026 and trades on Cboe BZX.

Is OPTO actively managed or an index fund?

OPTO is actively managed, so a portfolio manager chooses the holdings and their sizes instead of following an index.

Does OPTO pay a dividend?

Distribution schedule: Weekly (expected, not guaranteed). Distributions are the point of this fund, but they are not guaranteed and can include return of capital.

Why does OPTO use swaps?

Swaps let a fund get the return of a stock or index without holding the shares directly, which can be useful for foreign stocks or leverage. The trade-off is counterparty risk: the fund depends on the bank on the other side of the swap.

How do I buy OPTO?

OPTO trades like a stock on Cboe BZX. You can buy it in most brokerage accounts by searching the ticker OPTO. Newer, smaller ETFs can have wide bid-ask spreads, so many investors use limit orders.

Is OPTO a good investment?

We do not make buy or sell recommendations. Compare its strategy, fees and size with the other photonics ETFs and read the prospectus before deciding.
