LYTE at a glance

The Roundhill Photonics & Optics ETF focuses on the optical side of the AI infrastructure build: optical interconnects, lasers and photonic chips that move data with light inside AI data centers. At launch its top holdings included Lumentum, Coherent, Ciena and Fabrinet, plus China-listed optical module makers such as Eoptolink and Zhongji Innolight.

  • Size: about $109M in assets, #3 of 9 photonics ETFs, or roughly 16% of all the money in the funds we track.
  • Cost: 0.65% a year, cheaper than 4 of the other 8 funds. The group average is 0.69%.
  • How it is run: Actively managed.
  • Concentration: the three largest positions add up to about 60% of the fund (Lumentum, Coherent, Ciena).
  • Track record: 4 months of trading history, so there is no long-term record to judge yet.

How LYTE invests

LYTE puts at least 80% of its assets in companies whose core technology involves generating, manipulating, detecting or transmitting light. In practice it is a tight portfolio of the biggest optical names tied to AI data centers, with only about 18 positions.

It reaches the China-listed transceiver makers (Zhongji Innolight, Eoptolink and Suzhou TFC Optical) through total return swaps instead of buying the local shares directly. That is why its holdings file shows a large Treasury bill position: the bills back the swaps.

Roundhill says turnover should generally be limited to quarterly rebalances. The fund is non-diversified, which means a few names can make up a large share of the portfolio.

Read the prospectus and the latest holdings on the Roundhill website.

What LYTE holds

The top three positions make up about 60% of LYTE. Its published top holdings overlap most with PHOX (5 shared names), FOTO (4 shared names), EUV (3 shared names). See every stock the photonics ETFs own and how much they overlap.

LYTE fees

LYTE charges 0.65% a year. On a $10,000 position that is about $65 a year, $325 over five years and $650 over ten, before any gains or losses. The same $10,000 in the cheapest photonics ETF (EUV at 0.35%) would cost $350 over ten years, and the most expensive (LUMA at 1.00%) would cost $1,000.

LYTE size

With about $109M in assets, LYTE ranks #3 of 9 photonics ETFs. That is a mid-sized fund for this young category: big enough to trade without much trouble, but still far from the multi-billion dollar funds in broader tech.

Who LYTE may fit

It may fit if

  • You want the most concentrated bet on the largest AI optics names
  • You want exposure to the Chinese transceiver makers that supply much of the industry
  • You want a fund from a large thematic issuer with more trading volume than the smallest funds

It may not fit if

  • You want to avoid China exposure
  • You want a wide basket; a handful of names drive most of the return
  • You want an index fund with fixed rules

General education only, not a recommendation. Your own goals, timeline and risk tolerance decide what fits.

LYTE risks to understand

  • Three or four positions can make up more than half of the exposure
  • China-listed holdings add currency, policy and export-control risk
  • Swap exposure adds counterparty risk
  • Like every fund on this site, LYTE is a new, single-theme ETF. Photonics stocks fell hard in October 2026, with several of the biggest names dropping 10% or more in a day. Read our guide to photonics ETF risks before sizing a position.

LYTE FAQ

What is LYTE?

LYTE is the Roundhill Photonics & Optics ETF, a photonics-focused ETF from Roundhill. The Roundhill Photonics & Optics ETF focuses on the optical side of the AI infrastructure build: optical interconnects, lasers and photonic chips that move data with light inside AI data centers. At launch its top holdings included Lumentum, Coherent, Ciena and Fabrinet, plus China-listed optical module makers such as Eoptolink and Zhongji Innolight.

What is LYTE's expense ratio?

LYTE charges 0.65% a year, about $65 for every $10,000 invested. That ranks #4 of 9 photonics ETFs, cheapest first.

How big is LYTE?

LYTE has about $109M in assets under management, #3 of 9 photonics ETFs by size.

When did LYTE launch?

LYTE launched on August 6, 2026 and trades on Cboe BZX.

What does LYTE hold?

As of Oct 30, 2026, the largest positions were Lumentum, Coherent, Ciena, Zhongji Innolight, Eoptolink. Stock plus swap exposure combined. The Treasury bill held as swap collateral is left out. Holdings change, so check the issuer's website for the current list.

Is LYTE actively managed or an index fund?

LYTE is actively managed, so a portfolio manager chooses the holdings and their sizes instead of following an index.

Does LYTE pay a dividend?

Distribution schedule: Annual. Photonics companies mostly reinvest their cash, so growth-focused photonics ETFs pay little or nothing. Check the issuer's distribution history for actual payments.

Why does LYTE use swaps?

Swaps let a fund get the return of a stock or index without holding the shares directly, which can be useful for foreign stocks or leverage. The trade-off is counterparty risk: the fund depends on the bank on the other side of the swap.

Which photonics ETF is most similar to LYTE?

Based on published top holdings, LYTE shares the most names with PHOX (Lumentum, Coherent, Zhongji Innolight, Eoptolink). You can compare them side by side on our LYTE vs PHOX page.

How do I buy LYTE?

LYTE trades like a stock on Cboe BZX. You can buy it in most brokerage accounts by searching the ticker LYTE. Newer, smaller ETFs can have wide bid-ask spreads, so many investors use limit orders.

Is LYTE a good investment?

We do not make buy or sell recommendations. Compare its strategy, fees and size with the other photonics ETFs and read the prospectus before deciding.
