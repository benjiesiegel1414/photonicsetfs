PRAM at a glance

The Defiance Memory & Photonics ETF is a hybrid, not a pure photonics fund. It tracks the Solactive Memory and Photonics Index: the 20 largest eligible memory and photonics companies, equal-weighted at each quarterly rebalance. Coherent, Lumentum, Zhongji Innolight and Applied Optoelectronics sit next to Micron, SK Hynix, Seagate and Western Digital.

  • Size: about $5M in assets, #6 of 9 photonics ETFs.
  • Cost: 0.65% a year, cheaper than 4 of the other 8 funds. The group average is 0.69%.
  • How it is run: Index (passive), tracking the Solactive Memory and Photonics Index.
  • Concentration: the three largest positions add up to about 18% of the fund (Coherent, Broadcom, Astera Labs).
  • Track record: 3 months of trading history, so there is no long-term record to judge yet.
Uses swapsPart or all of the exposure comes through total return swaps with a bank instead of shares held directly.
Hybrid themePhotonics is only part of the fund.

Worth knowing

Roughly half of PRAM is memory and data storage, so it will not track the optical stocks as closely as the pure photonics funds. The fund gets its index exposure through a total return swap, not by holding the 20 stocks directly.

How PRAM invests

Defiance pitches PRAM as the data layer of AI: the memory that stores information and the optics that move it. The index picks the 20 largest companies across those two groups and gives each roughly a 5% weight when it rebalances every quarter.

Equal weighting means a smaller name such as Applied Optoelectronics starts each quarter with the same weight as Broadcom or Micron. That gives more punch to the smaller companies than a market-cap-weighted index would.

Instead of buying the stocks, the fund holds a swap on the whole index plus a government money market fund as collateral. The issuer's holdings page shows just three line items.

Read the prospectus and the latest holdings on the Defiance website.

What PRAM holds

The top three positions make up about 18% of PRAM. Its published top holdings overlap most with PHOX (3 shared names), LAZR (1 shared name), EUV (1 shared name). See every stock the photonics ETFs own and how much they overlap.

PRAM fees

PRAM charges 0.65% a year. On a $10,000 position that is about $65 a year, $325 over five years and $650 over ten, before any gains or losses. The same $10,000 in the cheapest photonics ETF (EUV at 0.35%) would cost $350 over ten years, and the most expensive (LUMA at 1.00%) would cost $1,000.

PRAM size

With about $5M in assets, PRAM ranks #6 of 9 photonics ETFs. That is small. Small funds can trade with wider bid-ask spreads, and issuers sometimes close funds that do not gather assets, so it is worth checking the spread and using limit orders.

Who PRAM may fit

It may fit if

  • You want photonics and memory together in one rules-based fund
  • You like equal weighting so no single stock dominates
  • You want a published index instead of a manager's picks

It may not fit if

  • You want pure photonics exposure; about half the index is memory and storage
  • You want the fund to hold the underlying shares directly
  • You want a large, heavily traded fund

General education only, not a recommendation. Your own goals, timeline and risk tolerance decide what fits.

PRAM risks to understand

  • Memory chips are among the most cyclical parts of the semiconductor industry
  • All of the exposure runs through a swap with a counterparty
  • Small asset base
  • Like every fund on this site, PRAM is a new, single-theme ETF. Photonics stocks fell hard in October 2026, with several of the biggest names dropping 10% or more in a day. Read our guide to photonics ETF risks before sizing a position.

PRAM FAQ

What is PRAM?

PRAM is the Defiance Memory & Photonics ETF, a photonics-focused ETF from Defiance. The Defiance Memory & Photonics ETF is a hybrid, not a pure photonics fund. It tracks the Solactive Memory and Photonics Index: the 20 largest eligible memory and photonics companies, equal-weighted at each quarterly rebalance. Coherent, Lumentum, Zhongji Innolight and Applied Optoelectronics sit next to Micron, SK Hynix, Seagate and Western Digital.

What is PRAM's expense ratio?

PRAM charges 0.65% a year, about $65 for every $10,000 invested. That ranks #5 of 9 photonics ETFs, cheapest first.

How big is PRAM?

PRAM has about $5M in assets under management, #6 of 9 photonics ETFs by size.

When did PRAM launch?

PRAM launched on August 26, 2026 and trades on Cboe BZX.

What does PRAM hold?

As of Nov 20, 2026, the largest positions were Coherent, Broadcom, Astera Labs, Everpure, Seagate. Index weights. The fund holds a swap on the index, not the stocks themselves. Holdings change, so check the issuer's website for the current list.

Is PRAM actively managed or an index fund?

PRAM is an index fund. It tracks the Solactive Memory and Photonics Index.

Does PRAM pay a dividend?

Distribution schedule: Quarterly. Photonics companies mostly reinvest their cash, so growth-focused photonics ETFs pay little or nothing. Check the issuer's distribution history for actual payments.

Why does PRAM use swaps?

Swaps let a fund get the return of a stock or index without holding the shares directly, which can be useful for foreign stocks or leverage. The trade-off is counterparty risk: the fund depends on the bank on the other side of the swap.

Which photonics ETF is most similar to PRAM?

Based on published top holdings, PRAM shares the most names with PHOX (Coherent, Broadcom, Credo Technology). You can compare them side by side on our PRAM vs PHOX page.

How do I buy PRAM?

PRAM trades like a stock on Cboe BZX. You can buy it in most brokerage accounts by searching the ticker PRAM. Newer, smaller ETFs can have wide bid-ask spreads, so many investors use limit orders.

Is PRAM a good investment?

We do not make buy or sell recommendations. Compare its strategy, fees and size with the other photonics ETFs and read the prospectus before deciding.
